Surah Al-Kafirun (Chapter 109 of the Quran): "The Disbelievers"

Note for adaptation: This is Quranic content, not a story with a human protagonist. Do not invent a character or narrative around a person. Every scene should be symbolic and figure-free: Arabic calligraphy of the verse, the Kaaba's exterior seen respectfully from a distance, a mosque, the night sky, geometric Islamic patterns, desert or Meccan landscape, light. Do not depict the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) or the disbelievers of Makkah as human figures at any point. The translation given below must be kept accurate; do not alter its meaning when adapting it into narration.

Context of revelation: In the early years of Islam in Makkah, the leaders of the Quraysh tried many times to make the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) compromise on his message. One of their offers was this: they proposed that the Prophet worship their idols for one year, and in return, they would worship Allah alone for one year. It was an offer of compromise on the very foundation of faith. In response, Allah revealed this short but powerful surah, a complete and firm rejection of any compromise in belief, while also establishing a principle of peaceful coexistence and religious boundaries.

The six verses and their meaning:

Verse 1: "Qul ya ayyuhal kafirun" — Say, O you who disbelieve.

Verse 2: "La a'budu ma ta'budun" — I do not worship what you worship.

Verse 3: "Wala antum 'abiduna ma a'bud" — Nor are you worshippers of what I worship.

Verse 4: "Wala ana 'abidun ma 'abadtum" — Nor will I ever worship what you worship.

Verse 5: "Wala antum 'abiduna ma a'bud" — Nor will you ever worship what I worship.

Verse 6: "Lakum deenukum wa liya deen" — For you is your religion, and for me is my religion.

The lesson: This surah teaches that faith cannot be compromised or diluted for the sake of convenience or social pressure. At the same time, it does not call for hatred or conflict — it draws a clear, peaceful boundary: everyone is free to follow their own belief, and no one should be forced or pressured to abandon their principles. It is a declaration of sincerity in worship and respectful coexistence with those who believe differently.
