The Three Men Trapped in the Cave

Long ago, three men were traveling together when a heavy storm forced them to take shelter in a cave in the mountains.

As they settled inside, a huge boulder rolled down from above and sealed the entrance completely.

The men were trapped in total darkness, with no way to push the rock away.

After trying everything, one of them said: "Nothing will save us now except if we call upon Allah by mentioning our most sincere good deeds."

The first man began. "O Allah," he said, "I had elderly parents, and every night I would milk our animals and bring them milk before anyone else in the household, even before my own children.

One night, I returned late after searching far for grazing land. I found my parents already asleep.

I did not want to wake them, but I also could not bring myself to give the milk to my children before my parents had their share.

So I stood there all night, the bowl in my hands, waiting for them to wake on their own, while my children cried at my feet from hunger.

When dawn came, my parents finally woke and drank the milk.

O Allah, if I did this only to seek Your pleasure, then relieve us from this hardship."

At that moment, the rock shifted slightly, and a thin ray of light entered the cave. But the opening was still too small to escape.

The second man spoke next. "O Allah," he said, "there was a woman I loved deeply, more than anyone I had ever known.

One day, I finally had the chance to be alone with her, and I sought to do wrong. But she said to me, 'Fear Allah, and do not break this seal except by right.'

So I stepped back, and I left her, even though I wanted her more than anything in the world.

O Allah, if I left her only out of fear of You, then relieve us from this hardship."

The rock shifted again, opening a little more. Yet still, they could not leave the cave.

The third man then spoke. "O Allah," he said, "I once hired a worker and paid him his wages, but he left without collecting his due.

I took his small wage and invested it, and over time it grew into great wealth, herds of livestock and more.

Years later, he came back to me and said, 'Servant of Allah, give me my wages.'

I told him, 'All that you see, the herds and the wealth, all of it is your due.'

He said, 'Do not mock me.' I replied, 'I am not mocking you,' and I gave him everything.

O Allah, if I did this only seeking Your pleasure, then relieve us fully from this hardship."

At once, the boulder rolled completely away, and the three men walked out of the cave into the light, free at last.

And that is the lesson of this story: sincerity, doing good only for the sake of Allah, without seeking praise or reward from people, is what truly moves mountains.

So today, look at your own good deeds, and ask yourself: are they for people to see, or are they sincerely for Allah alone?
